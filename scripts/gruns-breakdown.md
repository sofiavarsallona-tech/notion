# Grüns: the gummy was never the product

*Brand breakdown script, draft 2 (14 Oct 2026)*

---

## Hook (pick one)

1. **Unilever paid a reported $1.2 billion for a gummy. The gummy is the least interesting part.**
2. The founder of Grüns never asked "who's my customer?" He asked "who's going to buy my company?"
3. Grüns isn't a vitamin brand. It's a company that was built to be sold.

---

## Script (~70 sec)

Grüns wasn't the first vitamin brand. Not even close.
And yet Unilever bought it this year, for a price reported at $1.2 billion.

The reason has very little to do with what's inside the pack.

**One. He named the real buyer.**

We've all been taught the same first question: *who is your customer?*
Chad Janis asked a different one.

Before Grüns, he was a private equity investor. He sat on the boards of Dr. Squatch, the soap, and Brooklinen, the sheets. His job was to watch consumer brands grow, and to watch who bought them in the end.

And he learned one thing. Giant companies are terrible at inventing. They're too big and too careful. So they don't build the exciting brand. They wait for someone else to prove it works, and then they buy it.

So he started from the last page. Not "who's going to shop for this?" but "who's going to buy this company?"
This year, the answer was Unilever.

**Two. He took a position nobody held.**

The market had two doors.
A green powder: serious, expensive, and it tastes like a lawn.
Or gummy vitamins: cheap, pleasant, and nobody takes them seriously.

Grüns didn't split the difference. It took the price of the serious one and the pleasure of the cheap one. About $60 to $80 a month, much closer to AG1 than to a $20 bottle of gummies.
It charges like a supplement and tastes like candy.

**Three. He sold the feeling of health.**

Read the pack. 60+ ingredients. Whole-food vegetables. Adaptogens. Super mushrooms. Vegan. Gluten-free. And everything is green.
No single word proves it's good for you. Put together, they make you *feel* it is.
Nutrition-wise, that's up for debate. Branding-wise, it's genius.
It doesn't have to be the healthiest thing you take. It has to feel like the most responsible thing you do before 9am.

**Close.**

He named the buyer. He took a position nobody held. He sold the feeling, not the formula.

And this is the part for you.
Whatever you're building, whether it's a brand, a career or a body of work, don't stop at "who will like this?"
Ask where it ends. Then build it backwards from there.
